# How to Learn the Words in This Book

There are about 1,200 Spanish words in these pages. You will not learn them by reading them. Nobody does.
You learn a word by **trying to remember it, failing, and checking** — and then doing that again a week
later. That feels worse than re-reading. It works about three times better.

Here is the whole method. It takes fifteen minutes a day.

## The drill: look — say — cover — write — check

For each new word in a unit's vocabulary table:

1. **Look** at the Spanish and its English. Read the example sentence — that is where the word actually lives.
2. **Say** it out loud, once. Spanish spelling tells you exactly how; trust it.
3. **Cover** the Spanish column with a bookmark or your hand.
4. **Write** the Spanish from the English — the whole thing: **el** or **la**, every accent, every letter.
5. **Check.** If you were wrong, don't just look at the right answer and move on. Cover it and write it
   again, correctly, twice.

Work in batches of **five to seven words**, not twenty. Small batches come round faster, and coming round
is the point.

## Making cards from the glossary

The glossary at the back is built to be cut up. Photocopy the pages, cut between the columns, and fold each
strip so the English hides. Or write cards: **the Spanish and its little phrase on one side, the English on
the other.** Always put the phrase on the card, not the bare word — you will never need *la cuenta* on its
own; you will need *¿Me trae la cuenta, por favor?*

Then use **five envelopes, or five paperclipped piles**. Every new card starts in pile 1.

| Pile | Review it |
|---|---|
| 1 | every day |
| 2 | every 2 days |
| 3 | every 4 days |
| 4 | once a week |
| 5 | once a fortnight |

Got it right? The card moves up one pile. Got it wrong? **Straight back to pile 1**, however far it had
got. That is the whole system, and it is why you can do this without an app.

> **Paper cards beat apps here, and that is not nostalgia.** Across 32 studies of vocabulary cards, paper
> outperformed digital, and *ready-made* cards outperformed ones learners built themselves. So cut up the
> glossary rather than agonising over building a perfect deck.

## One true sentence about you

Once a unit, pick **three words you met two or three units ago** — not this unit's words; ones that have had
time to go cold. Write one sentence with each that is **true about your own life**.

Not *"El gato está en la mesa."* That is a sentence about a cat. Write *"Mi hermana vive en Lyon y no le
gusta el frío."*

True sentences stick because you already know what they mean and you will have a reason to say them again.
Three a unit is sixty by the end of the book. Keep them together on one page — that page is the most useful
thing you will write.

> **Do this with old words only.** When a word is brand new, your attention is still busy holding its shape
> and spelling, and building a sentence pulls that attention away. Give it a few weeks first.

## How often to come back

The book brings words back for you. The **Repaso relámpago** strip at the top of each Practice section, the
**Repaso** checkpoints, and the recycled questions marked in each test are all deliberately made of things
you learned earlier. **Do them with the book closed, and don't look back first.** Getting one wrong is not a
failure — it is the most useful thing that can happen to you in fifteen minutes, because a word you have
just failed to remember and then looked up is a word you will remember next time.

Your rhythm:

- **Every day** — pile 1, plus whatever piles are due.
- **Every unit** — the relámpago strip, cold. The five práctica exercises. Three true sentences.
- **After every unit** — wait **at least one day**, then take the test. Closed book, one sitting.
- **At each checkpoint** — the cumulative review. Anything you miss, go back to the unit it names.
- **One month after you finish the book** — come back and redo the last four units and Repaso 4. Those
  words are the ones the book had no room left to bring back for you. That one afternoon is worth more than
  any other afternoon you will spend with this book.

## One honest word about how much you will learn

This book puts about 1,200 words in front of you. You will not finish able to *use* all 1,200, and no
326-page book could do that — the research on deliberate vocabulary learning caps a course of this length
at somewhere around 500 to 750 words genuinely owned.

What you should expect is roughly this: **several hundred words you can produce yourself in a sentence, and
several hundred more you will recognise instantly when you read or hear them.** Both of those are real
knowledge. They are just not the same knowledge, and a book that promises you the first for everything on
its word list is not telling you the truth.
