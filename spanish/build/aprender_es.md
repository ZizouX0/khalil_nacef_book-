# How to Learn the Words in This Book

There are about 1,200 Spanish words in these pages, and reading them will teach you none of them.

You learn a word by **trying to remember it, failing, and checking** — then doing that again a week later. It
feels worse than re-reading. It works about three times better. That gap, between how a method feels and how
well it works, is most of why people study badly.

Fifteen minutes a day. Here is all of it.

## The drill: look — say — cover — write — check

For each new word in a unit's vocabulary table:

1. **Look** at the Spanish, its English, and the example sentence. The sentence is where the word lives.
2. **Say** it out loud, once. Spanish spelling tells you exactly how; trust it.
3. **Cover** the Spanish column with a bookmark or your hand.
4. **Write** the Spanish from the English — the whole thing: **el** or **la**, every accent, every letter.
5. **Check.** Wrong? Don't read the right answer and move on. Cover it and write it out correctly, twice.

Five to seven words at a time, not twenty. Small batches come round faster, and coming round is the point.
Seven is about my limit before the first ones start to blur.

## Making cards from the glossary

The glossary at the back is built to be cut up. Photocopy those pages, cut down between the columns, fold
each strip so the English hides. Or write your own: **the Spanish and its little phrase on one side, the
English on the other.**

Always the phrase, never the bare word. You will never need *la cuenta* on its own; you will need *¿Me trae
la cuenta, por favor?*

Then **five envelopes, or five paperclipped piles**. Every new card starts in pile 1.

| Pile | Review it |
|---|---|
| 1 | every day |
| 2 | every 2 days |
| 3 | every 4 days |
| 4 | once a week |
| 5 | once a fortnight |

Right, and it moves up a pile. Wrong, and it goes **straight back to pile 1**, however far it had climbed.
That is the whole system.

> **Paper beats apps here, and that is not nostalgia.** Across 32 studies of vocabulary cards, paper
> outperformed digital, and *ready-made* cards outperformed the ones learners built for themselves. So cut up
> the glossary instead of spending a Sunday building the perfect deck.

## One true sentence about you

Once a unit, pick **three words you met two or three units ago** — not this unit's, ones that have had time
to go cold. Write a sentence with each that is **true about your own life**.

Not *"El gato está en la mesa."* That is a sentence about a cat. Write *"Mi hermana vive en Lyon y no le
gusta el frío."*

True sentences stick: you already know what they mean, and you will have a reason to say them again. Three a
unit is sixty by the end of the book. Keep them on one page. That page will be the most useful thing you
write all year.

> **Old words only.** While a word is still new, your attention is busy holding its shape and its spelling,
> and building a sentence pulls that attention away. Give it a few weeks.

## How often to come back

The book brings the words back for you whether you plan for it or not. The **Repaso relámpago** strip, the
**Repaso** checkpoints, the recycled questions marked in every test — all deliberately made of things you
learned earlier. **Do them with the book shut, and don't look back first.**

Getting one wrong is not failure. It is the method, working.

Your rhythm:

- **Every day** — pile 1, plus whatever piles are due.
- **Every unit** — the relámpago strip, cold. The five práctica exercises. Three true sentences.
- **After every unit** — wait **at least one day**, then take the test. Closed book, one sitting.
- **At each checkpoint** — the cumulative review. Anything you miss, go back to the unit it names.
- **One month after you finish the book** — redo the last four units and Repaso 4. Those are the words the
  book ran out of room to bring back for you. That afternoon is worth more than any other you will spend with
  this book.

## How much of it will actually stick

Not all of it.

You will not finish able to *use* 1,200 words, and no 326-page book could get you there. The research on
deliberate vocabulary learning caps a course of this length at somewhere around 500 to 750 words genuinely
owned.

Expect this instead: several hundred words you can produce yourself in a sentence, and several hundred more
you recognise instantly when you read or hear them. Both are real knowledge. They are not the same knowledge,
and a book that promises you the first for everything on its word list is lying to you.
