# Which Spanish Are You Learning?

One language, about 500 million speakers, 21 countries, and they all understand each other. The differences
are real, though, and a book that pretends otherwise is doing you no favours.

**This book teaches the Spanish of Spain.** That's the variety of the course it's built on, and if you learn
it, everyone from Tijuana to Tierra del Fuego will understand you.

## Four things to know from day one

**1. "You all" is not the same everywhere.**
Spain uses **vosotros** informally and **ustedes** formally. **All of Latin America uses only *ustedes***, for
everyone, always. That's why every verb table in this book carries both rows. Heading to Latin America?
Ignore the *vosotros* row and you will never once be wrong.

**2. Vos.**
In Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay, most of Central America and parts of Colombia, informal "you" is **vos**,
not *tú*, and it comes with its own verb forms: *vos tenés, vos hablás, vos sos*. The tables show `tú (vos)`
so you recognise it when you meet it. You never have to produce it.

**3. The "th" sound.**
In most of Spain, **c** before *e/i* and **z** sound like English *th*: *cinco* = THEEN-ko, *plaza* =
PLA-tha. In all of Latin America and across southern Spain they are a plain **s**. That is called *seseo*,
and nobody apologises for it. Both are completely correct.

**4. About 5% of everyday words split.**
Each unit shows you the splits that matter in an **En España / En América** box.

|---|---|
| el coche | el carro, el auto |
| el ordenador | la computadora |
| el móvil | el celular |
| la patata | la papa |
| el zumo | el jugo |
| el billete (bus, train) | el boleto, el pasaje |

| coger (to take/catch) | tomar — *coger is vulgar in much of Latin America; use **tomar*** |

> **¡Ojo!** *Coger el autobús* is completely normal in Spain and badly rude in Mexico, Argentina and much of
> the region. When in doubt anywhere in the Americas, say **tomar**. If you memorise one word difference
> today, make it this one.

**Nothing you learn here will be "wrong" anywhere.** You will just sound like you learned in Madrid.
