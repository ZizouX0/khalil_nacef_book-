<p class="lead">Every unit ends with a test. This page tells you how to mark it and what your score means.
Read it once, now, before you take your first test — then come back here each time.</p>

## How to mark yourself

**Mark honestly, and mark afterwards.** Answer every question first. Then mark. Do not award yourself a
point for an answer you "meant" or "nearly had" — an answer you could not produce on the page is an answer
you could not produce in a conversation. Marking yourself strictly is the whole value of this page.

**Sections A–D (one point each).** The answer is either the one in the key or it is wrong. Where the key
shows two answers separated by a slash, either one earns the point.

**Spelling and accents.** A missing or wrong accent does not cost you a point at **A1** — unless the accent
is the thing being tested (*¿cuánto?*, *él*, *está*, and any verb form where the accent shows the person).
A wrong letter is a wrong answer: *tenedor* is right, *tenador* is not. In Section A, the wrong article
(*el* instead of *la*) loses the point. **From A2 on, accents count everywhere.** An accent is part of the
spelling of a Spanish word.

**Question marks.** A missing ¿ or ¡ does not cost you a point at A1. At A2 it counts as a slip.

**Section E — translation (two points each).**

- **2 points** — you used the structure the sentence was testing, and there is nothing else in your
  sentence that changes the meaning.
- **1 point** — you used the structure being tested, but there is one other slip: a wrong ending, a missing
  article, a wrong gender, a misspelling.
- **0 points** — the structure being tested is wrong or missing, or you left the sentence out.

The key names the structure being tested for each sentence, so you always know which part must be right.
**Your wording may differ from the key and still be correct.** Spanish allows more than one good
translation. If your sentence uses the tested structure correctly and a Spanish speaker would understand it
as the same message, give yourself the 2 points. If you are genuinely unsure whether your version works,
score it 1.

**The writing task (Section F at A1, Section G at A2).** There is no single right answer here, so you do not
compare your text word for word with the model. The model in the key is one good answer, not the only one.
Score yourself with the checks printed under the model: read your own text once for each check, and tick it
only if that thing is actually there on your page. One point per check. If a check is "nearly" there, it is
not ticked.

**The reading task (Section F at A2).** Questions answered in Spanish earn the point if the information is
right, even if the Spanish is imperfect — this section tests whether you understood the text.

## What your score means

| A1 test (out of 40) | A2 test (out of 50) | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **36–40** | **45–50** | **Excellent.** You own this unit. Go straight on. Put the two or three items you missed on a card and look at them again in a week. |
| **32–35** | **40–44** | **Pass — move on.** This is the level you need before starting the next unit. First: find every question you got wrong in the routing table, re-read the section it names, and write the correct answer out once by hand. |
| **24–31** | **30–39** | **Not yet. Repair, then retake.** Don't start the next unit. Use the routing table, re-read each section it sends you to, then redo the unit's five Práctica exercises. Wait at least two days, then take the test again from the beginning without looking at the answers. Waiting is not lost time — the gap is what makes the second attempt stick. |
| **0–23** | **0–29** | **Go back and re-study the unit.** Work through the whole unit again — grammar, vocabulary and the dialogue, out loud. Then redo the five Práctica exercises. Wait three days and retake. Scoring low here is normal and it is not a verdict on you; it means the unit needed a second pass, which most units do. |

**One more check, whatever your total.** Look at each section on its own. If you scored less than half the
points in any single section, go back to that section's material even if your total was a pass. A high
total can hide one blind spot, and that blind spot will cost you in the next unit.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1 — go back if under** | 3 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 5 | 3 | — |
| **A2 — go back if under** | 3 | 5 | 3 | 3 | 5 | 3 | 4 |

> **Why the tests are harder than the exercises.** They are meant to be. Retrieving something with effort is
> what makes it stick; retrieving it easily changes almost nothing. A test you breeze through has taught you
> nothing you did not already have. Some items also come from *earlier* units without warning — that is
> deliberate too, and it is why you will still be able to use Unit 3 by the time you reach Unit 18.
