# How to use this book

This is the second half of the course. The first book teaches you; this one makes the teaching stick.

There is one chapter here for every unit of *Spanish for Beginners*. Do a chapter **after** you have finished
the unit and taken its test — not instead of the test, and not on the same day.

## What is different about these exercises

The exercises inside the course book practise the unit you have just read. These ones do that too, but most of
what they ask about comes from **units you finished weeks ago**.

That is deliberate, and it is the whole reason this book exists. A word you meet once, use once and never see
again is a word you will not have in a year. The research is blunt about it: what makes vocabulary stay is
meeting it again *after you have started to forget it*. Not re-reading it. Trying to remember it, failing, and
checking.

So a chapter will ask you for a word you met eight units ago and have not thought about since. You will get
some of them wrong. **That is the exercise working, not you failing.** The moment of not-quite-remembering is
where the learning happens; if every answer came easily, the book would be wasting your afternoon.

## How to work through a chapter

1. **Shut the course book.** Do not look anything up first. If you cannot remember a word, leave the line
   blank and come back at the end.
2. **Write the answers in.** There is a ruled line under every item for a reason — a gap you fill with a pen
   is worth about three you read and nod at.
3. **Do the whole chapter before you mark anything.** Marking as you go turns a test into a copying exercise.
4. **Mark it from the Answer Key at the back**, in a different colour if you can.
5. **Read the notes, including on the ones you got right.** Where a note follows an answer, it is the rule the
   item was testing. Getting something right for the wrong reason is a problem you will only catch here.
6. **Anything you got wrong, write out correctly, twice.** Then find it in the course book and read that page
   again. A wrong answer you simply cross out teaches nothing.

## How long a chapter takes

Between **twenty-five and forty minutes**, and it is meant to be done in one sitting. If you are consistently
finishing in ten, you are looking things up. If you are consistently past an hour, split the chapter in two
and do the second half the next day — a long session you dread is worse than a short one you actually do.

## What is in a chapter

Every chapter has the same shape, so you never have to work out what to do:

| Section | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Vocabulario** | Words from this unit, then words from four, eight and twelve units back |
| **Gramática** | The unit's grammar, plus one point from earlier that most people lose |
| **Lectura** | A short text built out of words you are supposed to know by now, and questions on it |
| **Escritura** | One thing to write, with a model answer and a checklist to mark yourself against |
| **Repaso general** | Everything mixed together, in no helpful order |

That last section is mixed on purpose. When exercises are grouped by topic you can answer them on autopilot,
because the heading has already told you which rule to use. Mixed practice feels worse and works better — you
have to decide *what kind of problem this is* before you can solve it, which is exactly what you have to do
when somebody speaks to you.

## One honest warning

This book cannot make you fluent and it cannot teach you to understand speech — nothing printed can. What it
can do is make sure that the twelve hundred words and hundred grammar points in the course book are still
there in six months, instead of the three hundred that would otherwise survive.

That is a narrow promise, and it is the one thing that most separates people who finish a beginner's course
from people who can still use it a year later.
