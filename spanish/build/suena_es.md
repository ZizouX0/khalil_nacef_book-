# Suena así boxes

## Las cinco vocales {#u=A1-0}
Spanish has exactly five vowel sounds. They never change, never blur, never shorten — not even in an unstressed syllable.

- la pizarra — pee-THAH-rrah
- el bolígrafo — boh-LEE-grah-foh
- el ordenador — ohr-deh-nah-DOHR
- la mochila — moh-CHEE-lah
- hola — OH-lah *(the h is silent, always)*
- gracias — GRAH-thyahs

**Par mínimo:** **mesa** (MEH-sah) *table* / **misa** (MEE-sah) *mass*.

**En voz alta:** say **a – e – i – o – u** five times through, each one the same short length, like five identical taps of a finger. Then say *mesa, misa, mesa, misa*. If a vowel drifts towards English *uh* or French *eu*, stop and start again slower.

## ¿Dónde está el golpe? {#u=A1-1}
Every Spanish word of two syllables or more has one strong beat. Get it wrong and you have said a different word.

- la profesión — proh-feh-SYOHN
- el/la periodista — peh-ryoh-DEES-tah
- el/la traductor/a — trah-dook-TOHR
- la universidad — oo-behr-see-DAHD *(v sounds like b)*
- alemán — ah-leh-MAHN
- francesa — frahn-THEH-sah

**Par mínimo:** **papa** (PAH-pah) *potato* / **papá** (pah-PAH) *dad*. Same letters; the accent moves the beat and changes the word.

**En voz alta:** say your own name, then *Me llamo…* and your surname, tapping the table once on each strong beat. French speakers: resist putting the beat at the end of the phrase.

## La jota {#u=A1-2}
Spanish **j**, and **g** before **e/i**, are one rough sound made at the back of the mouth: **kh**.

- jugar — khoo-GAHR
- viajar — byah-KHAHR
- el mensaje — mehn-SAH-kheh
- la página web — PAH-khee-nah wehb
- el idioma — ee-DYOH-mah
- el inglés — een-GLEHS

**Par mínimo:** **caja** (KAH-khah) *box* / **casa** (KAH-sah) *house*.

**En voz alta:** if you speak French, you already have this sound. Say the **r** of *rue* — then say it again with your voice switched off, so only the friction is left. That is the jota. Hold it for three seconds, then run through all six words above.

## R y rr {#u=A1-3}
One **r** is a single flick of the tongue tip. Double **rr** — and any **r** starting a word — is rolled.

- el río — RREE-oh *(rolled: it starts the word)*
- la cordillera — kohr-dee-YEH-rah
- la primavera — pree-mah-BEH-rah
- el invierno — een-BYEHR-noh *(hard b after n)*
- el desierto — deh-SYEHR-toh
- la montaña — mohn-TAH-nyah

**Par mínimo:** **cero** (THEH-roh) *zero* / **cerro** (THEH-rroh) *hill*.

**En voz alta:** say English *ladder, ladder, ladder* fast — that quick tongue flick is the Spanish single **r**. Now put it into *cero*. Then try *cerro*: same place, but let the tongue bounce. Weeks, not days.

## B = V {#u=A1-4}
**B** and **v** are the same sound in Spanish. There is no /v/. Your top teeth must never touch your bottom lip.

- los vaqueros — lohs bah-KEH-rohs
- el vestido — behs-TEE-doh
- llevar — yeh-BAHR
- barato — bah-RAH-toh
- la bufanda — boo-FAHN-dah *(hard d after n)*
- el abrigo — ah-BREE-goh

**Par mínimo:** **bolso** (BOHL-soh) *handbag* / **bolsa** (BOHL-sah) *bag*. The final vowel is the whole difference — never soften it.

**En voz alta:** rest a finger lightly across your lips and say *vestido, vaqueros, llevar*. If you feel your teeth arrive, you are speaking English or French.

## La eñe y la hache {#u=A1-5}
**Ñ** is one sound — French speakers have it free in *agneau*. **H** is always silent.

- el hermano — ehr-MAH-noh
- la hija — EE-khah
- el abuelo — ah-BWEH-loh
- la sobrina — soh-BREE-nah
- la pareja — pah-REH-khah
- simpático — seem-PAH-tee-koh

**Par mínimo:** **cana** (KAH-nah) *grey hair* / **caña** (KAH-nyah) *small beer*. Two sounds, not one-and-a-half: *ny* must be a single squeeze of the tongue against the roof of the mouth.

**En voz alta:** say French *montagne*, then *España*, then *caña*. Now read the six words above, and delete every **h** before your mouth opens.

## Un golpe por sílaba {#u=A1-6}
Spanish gives every syllable the same amount of room. English crushes the weak ones; Spanish does not.

- despertarse — dehs-pehr-TAHR-seh
- levantarse — leh-bahn-TAHR-seh
- ducharse — doo-CHAHR-seh
- desayunar — deh-sah-yoo-NAHR
- acostarse — ah-kohs-TAHR-seh
- el mediodía — meh-dyoh-DEE-ah

**Par mínimo:** **esta** (EHS-tah) *this* / **está** (ehs-TAH) *he/she is*. *Esta mañana* and *Está mañana* are different sentences.

**En voz alta:** clap once per syllable, evenly, for *me le-van-to a las sie-te*. Every clap must land on a full vowel. If a clap lands on nothing, you swallowed a syllable.

## Nunca "uh" {#u=A1-7}
English reduces unstressed vowels to *uh*. Spanish never does. Four **a**s in a word are four identical **a**s.

- la ensalada — ehn-sah-LAH-dah
- la patata — pah-TAH-tah
- el tomate — toh-MAH-teh
- el pescado — pehs-KAH-doh
- la cerveza — thehr-BEH-thah
- la tortilla — tohr-TEE-yah

**Par mínimo:** **pollo** (POH-yoh) *chicken* / **polo** (POH-loh) *ice lolly*. Order carefully. And note: for most speakers today *pollo* and *poyo* (a stone bench) sound identical — that is called *yeísmo*.

**En voz alta:** say *la ensalada* five times, holding each **a** for exactly the same beat. Record it once and listen for a weak one.

## Las palabras se enganchan {#u=A1-8}
Spanish is written with spaces but not spoken with them. A final consonant jumps onto the next vowel.

- la esquina — laes-KEE-nah *(la + es merge)*
- la iglesia — lahy-GLEH-syah
- la avenida — lah-beh-NEE-dah
- el barrio — ehl BAH-rryoh
- al lado de — ahl LAH-doh deh
- enfrente de — ehn-FREHN-teh deh

**Par mínimo:** **pero** (PEH-roh) *but* / **perro** (PEH-rroh) *dog*.

**En voz alta:** read this as one single long word, with no gaps at all: *El hombre está al lado del parque* → eh-LOHM-breh-ehs-TAH-ah-LAH-doh-dehl-PAHR-keh. That joining is why Spanish sounds fast. It is not fast; it is joined.

## P, t, c sin aire {#u=A1-9}
English **p, t, k** start with a puff of air. Spanish ones have none — they are dry and sharp.

- el/la peluquero/a — peh-loo-KEH-roh
- el/la policía — poh-lee-THEE-ah
- el/la taxista — tahk-SEES-tah
- conducir — kohn-doo-THEER
- trabajador/a — trah-bah-khah-DOHR
- paciente — pah-THYEHN-teh

**Par mínimo:** **peso** (PEH-soh) *weight* / **beso** (BEH-soh) *kiss*. No puff on the **p** — but do not let it slide into **b** either.

**En voz alta:** hold a strip of paper an inch from your lips. Say English *Peter* — it flutters. Say *peluquero* — it must not move at all.

## Las tres reglas del acento {#u=A2-1}
Ends in a vowel, **-n** or **-s** → beat on the second-to-last syllable. Any other ending → beat on the last. A written accent overrides both.

- el vocabulario — boh-kah-boo-LAH-ryoh
- la pronunciación — proh-noon-thyah-THYOHN
- la gramática — grah-MAH-tee-kah
- el examen — ehk-SAH-mehn *(plural* exámenes *— the accent appears)*
- el abecedario — ah-beh-theh-DAH-ryoh
- la vergüenza — behr-GWEHN-thah *(ü = gw)*

**Par mínimo:** **público** (POO-blee-koh) *public* / **publico** (poo-BLEE-koh) *I publish* / **publicó** (poo-blee-KOH) *he published*.

**En voz alta:** say the three in a row, tapping the table hard on the strong beat each time. Nothing else changes — only the beat.

## C, z y s {#u=A2-2}
In Spain, **z** and **c** before **e/i** are the *th* of English *think*. In Latin America they are plain **s**. Both are correct.

- nacer — nah-THEHR
- crecer — kreh-THEHR
- el éxito — EHK-see-toh
- la actriz — ahk-TREETH
- protagonizar — proh-tah-goh-nee-THAHR
- el largometraje — lahr-goh-meh-TRAH-kheh

**Par mínimo:** **casa** (KAH-sah) *house* / **caza** (KAH-thah) *hunt*. In Latin America these are identical and context does the work — which tells you how small the risk is either way.

**En voz alta:** read aloud, marking the beats first: *Nació en Madrid, creció en Sevilla y se casó en 1999.*

## La d suave {#u=A2-3}
Between vowels, **d** is not a hard *d*. The tongue tip sits between the teeth and lets air through — English *this*.

- el dormitorio — dohr-mee-TOH-ryoh
- la madera — mah-DEH-rah
- amueblado — ah-mweh-BLAH-doh
- acogedor/a — ah-koh-kheh-DOHR
- la estantería — ehs-tahn-teh-REE-ah
- la espalda — ehs-PAHL-dah *(hard d after l)*

**Par mínimo:** **cada** (KAH-dah) *each* / **cara** (KAH-rah) *face*. English speakers flap their **d**s, so these two collapse easily — keep them apart.

**En voz alta:** say *cada* — tongue tip forward, air leaking. Say *cara* — tongue taps once and leaves. Alternate ten times.

## La melodía de la pregunta {#u=A2-4}
In Spanish, tone alone can turn a statement into a question. There is no word order to help you.

- perdone — pehr-DOH-neh
- oiga — OHY-gah
- el/la vecino/a — beh-THEE-noh
- agradecer — ah-grah-deh-THEHR
- quejarse — keh-KHAHR-seh
- el cortado — kohr-TAH-doh

**Par mínimo:** **si** (see) *if* / **sí** (see) *yes*. Identical alone — but in a sentence *sí* takes a beat of its own and *si* leans on the next word. That is what the accent is telling you.

**En voz alta:** say *Un cortado, por favor* with the voice falling on **-VOR**. Now say *¿Un cortado, por favor?* with it rising. Same words, different act.

## Diptongo o hiato {#u=A2-5}
Two vowels together are usually one beat. A written accent on **í** or **ú** splits them into two.

- el aeropuerto — ah-eh-roh-PWEHR-toh
- la excursión — ehks-koor-SYOHN
- apuntarse — ah-poon-TAHR-seh
- disfrutar — dees-froo-TAHR
- la discoteca — dees-koh-TEH-kah
- el horario — oh-RAH-ryoh *(silent h)*

**Par mínimo:** **hay** (ahy) *there is* — one beat / **ahí** (ah-EE) *there* — two beats. The accent is doing the work.

**En voz alta:** say *Voy a ir a la playa* as one unbroken chain — BOY-ah-EER-ah-lah-PLAH-yah. Count the beats on your fingers, then say it again without stopping anywhere.

## La erre fuerte {#u=A2-6}
Kitchen verbs are full of **r**. Single **r** flicks once; **rr** and word-initial **r** roll.

- añadir — ah-nyah-DEER
- hervir — ehr-BEER *(silent h, v sounds like b)*
- freír — freh-EER *(two beats)*
- picar — pee-KAHR
- el arroz — ah-RROHTH
- la alcachofa — ahl-kah-CHOH-fah

**Par mínimo:** **pera** (PEH-rah) *pear* / **perra** (PEH-rrah) *female dog*. Worth getting right in a shop.

**En voz alta:** the roll takes months, and being understood does not depend on it — a single flick where a roll belongs is a small accent, not a breakdown. Practise anyway: relax the tongue, push air, let it bounce. Two minutes a day.

## El acento del pretérito {#u=A2-7}
In the past tense the beat moves to the end, and the written accent tells you so. Miss it and you are speaking in the present.

- la bahía — bah-EE-ah
- el paisaje — pahy-SAH-kheh
- las ruinas — RRWEE-nahs
- impresionante — eem-preh-syoh-NAHN-teh
- el templo — TEHM-ploh
- la selva — SEHL-bah

**Par mínimo:** **visito** (bee-SEE-toh) *I visit* / **visitó** (bee-see-TOH) *he/she visited*.

**En voz alta:** take any line from this unit's dialogue. Read it once with every verb in the present, then again with every verb in the preterite. Listen to the beat sliding one syllable to the right.

## Con la mano en la garganta {#u=A2-8}
Some sounds buzz and some do not. Your throat can tell you which, without any recording.

- la garganta — gahr-GAHN-tah
- la rodilla — rroh-DEE-yah *(rolled: it starts the word)*
- el estómago — ehs-TOH-mah-goh
- la muela — MWEH-lah
- el oído — oh-EE-doh *(two beats: the accent splits them)*
- la espalda — ehs-PAHL-dah

**Par mínimo:** **ojo** (OH-khoh) *eye* / **hoyo** (OH-yoh) *hole*.

**En voz alta:** put two fingers flat on your throat. Say *ojo* — the middle must be silent, no vibration at all. Say *hoyo* — it should buzz all the way through. That is the whole difference.

## El acento del imperfecto {#u=A2-9}
The imperfect puts an accent on **-ía** and splits the vowels into two beats. It is the difference between two tenses and two words.

- la dictadura — deek-tah-DOO-rah
- la democracia — deh-moh-KRAH-thyah
- la frontera — frohn-TEH-rah
- la juventud — khoo-behn-TOOD
- el siglo — SEE-gloh
- enriquecedor/a — ehn-rree-keh-theh-DOHR *(rolled r after n)*

**Par mínimo:** **hacia** (AH-thyah) *towards* — two beats / **hacía** (ah-THEE-ah) *he used to do* — three beats.

**En voz alta:** say *hacia*, tapping twice; say *hacía*, tapping three times. Then run the same test on *vivía, tenía, quería, sabía* — three beats each, accent on the **í**.

## Todo junto {#u=A2-10}
Last box. Everything at once: five clean vowels, the beat where the accent says, no gaps between words.

- asustarse — ah-soos-TAHR-seh
- de repente — deh rreh-PEHN-teh *(rolled r)*
- el terremoto — teh-rreh-MOH-toh
- emocionarse — eh-moh-thyoh-NAHR-seh
- la vergüenza — behr-GWEHN-thah
- el ruido — RRWEE-doh

**Par mínimo:** **coro** (KOH-roh) *choir* / **corro** (KOH-rroh) *I run*.

**En voz alta:** read this unit's anecdote aloud twice. The second time, record it on your phone. Then check three things only: did the beat land on your pencil marks, did any vowel go to *uh*, did you stop between words? Fix one and move on.
