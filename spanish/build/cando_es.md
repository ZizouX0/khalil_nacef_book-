# Can-do promises (one per unit)

## En el aula {#u=A1-0}
- Greet someone at nine in the morning, at four in the afternoon and at ten at night, and get all three right.
- Ask how somebody is, and answer when the question comes back at you.
- Say you are lost. Then get the other person to slow down, repeat, or find another way to say it.
- Ask what a thing is called in Spanish, ask how a word is spelled, and spell your own name out loud.
- Count to ten, and do what the teacher has just asked the class to do.

*None of that is grammar, and all of it is what keeps the class in Spanish.*

## Nosotros y nosotras {#u=A1-1}
- Introduce yourself in one breath: your name, your age, where you are from, what you do for a living.
- Do the same for the person standing next to you.
- Ask somebody all of that back, and follow the answer.
- Answer direct questions about yourself, as long as the other person is not in a hurry.
- Fill in a form without guessing at the boxes: name, nationality, age, phone number, address.
- Count to 100, which is all you need for your age and your own phone number.

## Quiero aprender español {#u=A1-2}
- Say which languages you speak, and how well, without overselling yourself.
- Give your reason for learning Spanish out loud, even when the real reason is a person.
- Talk about what you do in class and what you do for fun, in the present tense.
- Say what you enjoy about learning a language, and what you would happily never do again.
- Get *el/la/un/una* onto the right words, so people hear the thing you actually meant.

## ¿Dónde está Santiago? {#u=A1-3}
- Put a country, a region or a city on the map in Spanish, and say what it is near.
- Describe a place with the words you actually have: big, cold, mountainous, full of people.
- Say what the weather is doing right now, and what it does there in each season.
- Ask *where*, *how*, *how many* and *what's it like*, and understand the answer, whether it is said to you or printed under a map.

## ¿Cuál prefieres? {#u=A1-4}
- Buy something: say what you want, ask what it costs, and say which one you prefer.
- Point at things and ask about them — *this one, that one, which one?* — without knowing the word for the thing.
- Describe clothes and objects by colour, size and what they are made of, and hold two of them up against each other.
- Understand a price said out loud at speed, and handle numbers past a hundred.
- Say what you have to buy and what you have to do, with *tener que*.

## Tus amigos son mis amigos {#u=A1-5}
- Describe your family — who they are, how old, what they are like to be around — and say whose is whose with *mi, tu, su, nuestro*.
- Say what you like, what you love, and what you cannot stand.
- Agree and disagree with somebody else's taste (*a mí también / a mí tampoco / a mí sí*).
- Describe a person's character, then take the edge off it with *muy, bastante, un poco*.

## Día a día {#u=A1-6}
- Walk someone through your entire day, from the alarm going off to the light going out.
- Tell the time.
- Say at what time you do each thing, and how often: always, sometimes, never, twice a week.
- Put a day in order with *primero, después, luego, por último*.
- Ask somebody about their own routine, and understand what they tell you.

## ¡A comer! {#u=A1-7}
- Read a Spanish menu and find the thing you actually want on it.
- Order a starter, a main, a drink and something afterwards, without pointing at any of it.
- Ask what is in a dish before it arrives, and say what is in yours.
- Say how you want something cooked without naming the food twice.
- Ask for the bill, and ask what it comes to.

## El barrio ideal {#u=A1-8}
- Describe your neighbourhood: what it has, what it has not got, and what it has far too much of.
- Ask whether there is a chemist near here, and understand the answer, which is the harder half.
- Give directions and follow them: straight on, second on the left, opposite the church.
- Put a thing exactly where it is: next to, near, far from, behind, on the corner.
- Read the signs and notices on the street without stopping to translate them.

## ¿Sabes conducir? {#u=A1-9}
- Say what you know how to do, and ask somebody else the same.
- Talk about what you have already done and where you have already been (*he trabajado, he vivido, ¿has estado alguna vez…?*).
- Describe your job or your studies, and what it takes to do them.
- Get through the first five minutes of an interview: answer the questions, then ask two of your own.
- Describe yourself to somebody who has never met you.

## El español y tú {#u=A2-1}
- Say how long you have been learning Spanish, and how long you have been doing anything else (*desde, desde hace, hace… que*).
- Say what you find hard, what you enjoy, what makes your voice go quiet, and why you are doing this at all.
- Say what you do to learn, and ask another learner what actually works for them.

## Una vida de película {#u=A2-2}
- Tell the story of a life, out loud or on paper: born, studied, moved, started work, died.
- Put dates and years on it, and say when each thing happened.
- Say how long something lasted, and when it started and stopped (*desde, durante, hasta*).
- Describe things that happened, briefly, without stalling on every verb.

## Hogar, dulce hogar {#u=A2-3}
- Describe where you live, room by room, and what is in each one.
- Say exactly where a thing is, precisely enough that somebody else could go and fetch it.
- Compare two homes or two objects: bigger, smaller, dearer, the same as.
- Say which one is yours and which one is theirs (*el mío, la tuya, el suyo*).
- Say which you prefer, what you love about it, and why.

## ¿Cómo va todo? {#u=A2-4}
- Greet, introduce and take your leave, at the right pitch for whoever is in front of you.
- Ask for something politely, and ask somebody for a favour (*¿Puedes…? ¿Te importa…? ¿Podrías…?*).
- Say yes, say no, say sorry, and say something back when you are thanked or apologised to.
- Say what a person is doing this very minute (*está trabajando, estoy comiendo*).
- Lend things, borrow things, and ask for them back.

## Guía del ocio {#u=A2-5}
- Suggest something for tonight, and say yes or no to somebody else's suggestion.
- Pin the arrangement down: where, when and what time, reading the times, prices and places off a listings page if that is where they are.
- Say what you are going to do, what you want to do, and what you are only thinking about doing.
- Say what you have already done and what you have not got round to (*ya / todavía no*).

## No como carne {#u=A2-6}
- Say what you do not eat, and why, clearly enough that nobody has to guess.
- Explain how a dish is made, step by step, with the impersonal *se*, and follow a Spanish recipe somebody else wrote.
- Ask for a quantity at a counter: a kilo, half a litre, two hundred grams, a packet.
- Keep talking about the food without naming it again every time (*lo pongo, las corto, se añaden*).

## Nos gustó mucho {#u=A2-7}
- Say where you went, what you did there, and whether it was worth it.
- Tell apart something you have done at some point from something you did on one particular Tuesday.
- Give an opinion about a place, a film or a meal, and react to somebody else's (*¡Qué bonito! ¡Qué bien!*).
- Say what you would like to do (*me gustaría ir a…*).
- Say whether you got on with the people, and whether you enjoyed yourself.

## Estamos muy bien {#u=A2-8}
- Say what hurts, and ask somebody else what is wrong with them.
- Describe your symptoms at a pharmacy counter and ask for something that will help.
- Tell somebody what to do, in the imperative, without sounding like a traffic warden.
- Tell apart what a person is like from how they happen to be today (*es nervioso / está nervioso*).
- Wish somebody well, and find the right thing to say when the news is bad.

## Antes y ahora {#u=A2-9}
- Describe the life you used to have, and the people and places in it: where you lived, what you did, what you were into.
- Set then against now (*antes… ahora…, ya no…, todavía…*).
- Talk about the stages of your own life, and ask an older person about theirs.
- Write a handful of joined-up sentences about your childhood, or about something you used to do every day.

## Momentos especiales {#u=A2-10}
- Tell a story about something that happened to you, and get all the way to the end of it.
- Set the scene first, then let the thing happen (*hacía frío y estábamos cansados, y de repente…*).
- Say what you were in the middle of when you were interrupted.
- Hold the whole thing together with *primero, entonces, de repente, al final, y, pero, porque*.
- Say how you felt, and keep somebody else's story moving (*¿Sí? ¿De verdad? ¡Qué fuerte!*).

*The last unit, and more or less the point of the other nineteen.*
